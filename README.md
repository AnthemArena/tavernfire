# Tavernfire

A community site where tabletop RPG players and game masters swap ideas, started by three sparks.

> Working name for now. Domain and trademark checks are still needed. Earlier candidates: Hookbook, Rumourmill, Wordhoard, Fablehoard.

## The idea in one line

Someone posts three sparks and what they need. The community answers with something made by a person, and every answer stays in a library anyone can search.

## Vocabulary

- **Spark:** a single word that starts an idea (for example *Statue*, *Time*, *Glowing*). A request has three sparks.
- **Traits:** the author-chosen descriptors on an entry (for example *Ancient, Patient, Guardian*). Not the same thing as sparks.
- **Request:** three sparks plus the type of thing needed, with an optional note about the table or session.
- **Entry / answer:** one person's response to a request.
- **Collection:** a personal set of saved entries, for example per campaign.

## How it works

1. **Post a request.** Choose three sparks and a type (NPC or location at launch). Optionally add context, such as party level or tone.
2. **The community answers.** Each entry follows a short template: name, concept, up to five traits, and a one-line "How a GM could use it".
3. **Voting.** Entries are shown in random order. Authors and vote totals stay hidden until voting closes, so people vote on the idea and early leaders do not sway later voters.
4. **Reveal.** Authors are revealed and winners highlighted.
5. **Everything is kept.** Every entry stays filed under its three sparks and remains searchable and saveable.

### Request lifecycle

1. Request opens for answers (fixed window, about 3 days)
2. Submissions close
3. Voting period
4. Authors revealed, winners highlighted
5. Entries remain in the searchable library

## Discovery

Entries can be found two ways:

- **By spark.** Search one, two or all three sparks. Exact matches rank first, then partial matches. "Close matches" shows requests that share two of three sparks.
- **By trait.** Author-chosen, capped at about five per entry, with suggestions drawn from existing traits to avoid near-duplicates (Ancient, Old, Aged).

Sparks rank above traits in search, and the interface keeps the two visibly separate.

## Launch scope

Start narrow so entries pile up where people look.

**Launch**
- Request board with three-spark prompts
- NPC and location request types
- Optional session context
- Community answers with a consistent template
- Anonymous voting until the voting period closes
- Author profiles and credit after the reveal
- Search by spark and by trait
- Personal collections
- "I used this" self-reported counter
- Report entry and basic moderation

**Later**
- Duels and timed "blitz" modes (a second game on top of the first)
- Daily challenges and leaderboards
- Reputation levels and creator achievements
- More types: creatures, player characters, items, abilities, factions, spells, rumours, traps, plot hooks, adventure seeds
- Campaign-specific collections and sharing
- Better search and recommendations
- Optional system-specific stat blocks (the default stays system-neutral)

Because all types share the same sparks, one set of three can eventually yield a full set: the NPC, the place they guard, the item they carry and the rumour that leads there.

## Made by people

The site is for things made by people. This is a shared community standard, not a guarantee, because AI-written text cannot be reliably detected.

- Authors submit only their own work.
- Allowed: proofreading and spell-checking; using reference material for inspiration, with attribution where appropriate.
- Not allowed: asking an AI to generate or substantially rewrite the entry.
- **Report entry** (with reasons: AI-generated, plagiarism, spam, abuse) sends an entry to private moderator review. A report never penalises an author automatically.
- Authors can appeal.
- Account reputation builds over time; new accounts get more scrutiny.

## Seeding the library

An empty library is the biggest early risk, so it should be seeded deliberately.

- Look at actual-play shows and other tables for the kinds of things GMs need, then turn those needs into three-spark **requests**, not copied entries.
- Founding members write original answers to those requests. Seed entries are written by people and labelled as founding entries.
- Do not copy published creatures, named characters or stat blocks. Ideas and tropes are open; specific expression and trademarks are not. Get proper legal advice before launch.
- Folklore, mythology and medieval bestiaries are rich public-domain sources. Open game content (for example the D&D 5e SRD under Creative Commons, or Pathfinder's open licence) can be used under its own terms and attribution rules.
- Keep the seed small and strong: roughly 50 to 100 requests with a few good answers each, in the launch types.

## Licensing (to settle before public submissions)

Preferred direction: authors keep ownership, grant the site permission to display and distribute their entries, and other users receive a clear licence to use entries in personal tabletop games. Be explicit about attribution and whether commercial use (for example in a published adventure) is permitted.

## Validation plan

Before building much, run it by hand in a Discord server or subreddit with a founding group of about 15 to 30 people. A few requests a week, answers in replies, votes by poll, entries kept in a spreadsheet.

Measure:

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Requests receiving answers | Is there enough activity? |
| Time to first answer | Does it feel responsive? |
| Answers per request | Are people motivated to contribute? |
| Entries saved | Are the creations useful? |
| Entries used in games | Does it deliver on its promise? |
| Returning contributors | Is creating enjoyable enough to repeat? |

The key question is whether people come back to create something for somebody else's table.

## Open questions

- How strict the AI policy is for assisted writing
- Licensing and ownership terms
- Whether answers stay open for a fixed window only, or can also close on a vote threshold
- Which content types to add after launch, and in what order
